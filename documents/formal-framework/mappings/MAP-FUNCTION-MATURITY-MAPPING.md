## Overview

The MAP function establishes context to frame risks related to AI systems. It is the foundation for risk identification and assessment activities that feed into MEASURE and MANAGE functions.

**ISO 42001 Primary Mapping**:
- MAP aligns primarily with ISO 42001 **Clause 6 (Planning)**, specifically:
  - 6.1 Actions to address risks and opportunities
  - 6.2 AI system objectives and planning to achieve them
- Also connects to **Clause 4.1** (Understanding the organization and its context)
- And **Clause 4.2** (Understanding the needs and expectations of interested parties)

**MAP Function Structure**:
- **5 Categories**: Context, Categorization, Capabilities, Risks/Benefits, Impacts
- **14 Subcategories**: Detailed risk mapping requirements
- **Cross-cutting**: Informs GOVERN, feeds MEASURE and MANAGE

## Level 1: Initial - Ad-Hoc AI Context Understanding

**ISO 42001 Reference**: Gaps in Clauses 4.1, 4.2, and 6.1 - No systematic planning or context analysis for AI systems

### Overview
Organizations at Level 1 have no structured approach to understanding AI system context or mapping AI-specific risks. AI systems may be deployed without proper risk framing, context establishment, or stakeholder analysis.

---

### MAP 1: Context Establishment and Understanding

#### MAP 1.1: Intended Purposes, Beneficial Uses, Laws/Norms, and Deployment Settings
- **Status**: Undefined or undocumented
- **Characteristics**:
  - AI systems deployed without documented intended purpose
  - No identification of potentially beneficial uses
  - Context-specific laws and norms not analyzed
  - Deployment settings not formally documented
  - User types and expectations unknown or assumed
  - Potential positive/negative impacts not assessed
  - Assumptions and limitations not documented
  - TEVV metrics not defined

#### MAP 1.2: Interdisciplinary AI Actors with Diverse Competencies
- **Status**: Homogeneous or undefined teams
- **Characteristics**:
  - AI development by single discipline (e.g., only engineers)
  - No demographic diversity requirements
  - Limited domain expertise involvement
  - User experience not considered in design
  - Participation not documented
  - No prioritization of interdisciplinary collaboration

#### MAP 1.3: Organization's Mission and Goals for AI Technology
- **Status**: Not articulated or connected to AI initiatives
- **Characteristics**:
  - AI projects disconnected from organizational mission
  - Technology goals not documented
  - No strategic alignment assessment
  - AI used opportunistically without clear purpose

#### MAP 1.4: Business Value or Context of Business Use
- **Status**: Vague or undefined
- **Characteristics**:
  - No clear business case for AI systems
  - Value not quantified or articulated
  - Context not evaluated for existing systems
  - ROI not calculated or considered

#### MAP 1.5: Organizational Risk Tolerances
- **Status**: Not determined or documented
- **Characteristics**:
  - No defined risk appetite for AI systems
  - Risk tolerance varies by individual or project
  - No organizational risk tolerance statement
  - Risk decisions made ad-hoc

#### MAP 1.6: System Requirements and Socio-Technical Implications
- **Status**: Technical requirements only, if any
- **Characteristics**:
  - Requirements not elicited from stakeholders
  - Only functional requirements considered
  - Socio-technical implications ignored
  - No involvement of relevant AI actors
  - Design decisions made in isolation

---

### MAP 2: Categorization of AI System

#### MAP 2.1: Specific Tasks and Methods
- **Status**: Undefined or informally described
- **Characteristics**:
  - AI system tasks not formally specified
  - Methods (classifiers, generative models, recommenders) not categorized
  - Implementation approaches undocumented
  - No taxonomy for AI system types

#### MAP 2.2: Knowledge Limits and Human Oversight
- **Status**: Not documented
- **Characteristics**:
  - System knowledge limits unknown
  - How outputs will be utilized unclear
  - Human oversight not planned
  - Insufficient information for AI actor decision-making

#### MAP 2.3: Scientific Integrity and TEVV Considerations
- **Status**: Not identified or planned
- **Characteristics**:
  - No experimental design considerations
  - Data collection/selection not planned for integrity
  - Representativeness not assessed
  - Suitability of data not evaluated
  - System trustworthiness not considered
  - Construct validation not performed

---

### MAP 3: AI Capabilities, Usage, Goals, and Expected Benefits/Costs

#### MAP 3.1: Potential Benefits
- **Status**: Assumed but not examined
- **Characteristics**:
  - Benefits not formally documented
  - Intended functionality not validated
  - Performance expectations not established
  - No systematic examination of benefits

#### MAP 3.2: Potential Costs
- **Status**: Not examined
- **Characteristics**:
  - Monetary costs may be tracked, but non-monetary costs ignored
  - Impact of AI errors not considered
  - Trustworthiness costs not evaluated
  - No connection to organizational risk tolerance

#### MAP 3.3: Targeted Application Scope
- **Status**: Ill-defined or scope creep
- **Characteristics**:
  - Application scope not specified
  - System capability not documented
  - Context not considered in scoping
  - AI system categorization not performed

#### MAP 3.4: Operator/Practitioner Proficiency
- **Status**: Assumed or not assessed
- **Characteristics**:
  - No processes for assessing operator proficiency
  - Performance expectations not defined
  - Trustworthiness understanding not evaluated
  - Technical standards/certifications not identified

#### MAP 3.5: Human Oversight Processes
- **Status**: Undefined or ad-hoc
- **Characteristics**:
  - Human oversight not planned
  - Processes not defined or documented
  - Not aligned with organizational policies
  - Human roles in AI decision-making unclear

---

### MAP 4: Risks and Benefits for All Components Including Third-Party

#### MAP 4.1: Approaches for Mapping AI Technology and Legal Risks
- **Status**: Not in place
- **Characteristics**:
  - No systematic approach to risk mapping
  - Third-party data/software risks not assessed
  - Legal risks not identified
  - Intellectual property infringement risks not considered
  - Approaches not documented

#### MAP 4.2: Internal Risk Controls
- **Status**: Not identified
- **Characteristics**:
  - AI system component risks not identified
  - Third-party AI technology risks not assessed
  - Internal controls not documented
  - No risk control framework for AI

---

### MAP 5: Impacts to Individuals, Groups, Communities, Organizations, and Society

#### MAP 5.1: Likelihood and Magnitude of Impacts
- **Status**: Not identified or assessed
- **Characteristics**:
  - Beneficial impacts not identified
  - Harmful impacts not assessed
  - Likelihood not evaluated
  - Magnitude not estimated
  - Past uses not reviewed
  - Public incident reports not consulted
  - No feedback from external actors

#### MAP 5.2: Engagement with Relevant AI Actors
- **Status**: No practices in place
- **Characteristics**:
  - No regular engagement mechanisms
  - Feedback not integrated
  - Positive/negative impacts not tracked
  - Unanticipated impacts not captured
  - Personnel not assigned to engagement

## Level 3: Defined - Standardized AI Context Processes

**ISO 42001 Reference**: Substantial implementation of Clauses 4.1, 4.2, and 6.1 - Systematic context analysis and AI system planning in place

### Overview
Organizations at Level 3 have standardized, documented processes for establishing AI system context and mapping risks. All AI initiatives follow consistent practices for context establishment, risk identification, and stakeholder engagement.

---

### MAP 1: Context Establishment and Understanding

#### MAP 1.1: Intended Purposes, Beneficial Uses, Laws/Norms, and Deployment Settings
- **Status**: Comprehensively understood and documented
- **Characteristics**:
  - Intended purpose thoroughly documented for all AI systems
  - Potentially beneficial uses systematically identified and validated
  - Context-specific laws and norms comprehensively researched
  - Deployment settings fully characterized
  - All user types and expectations documented
  - Potential positive and negative impacts systematically assessed
  - All assumptions and limitations explicitly documented
  - TEVV and system metrics defined and validated
  - Documentation maintained throughout lifecycle

#### MAP 1.2: Interdisciplinary AI Actors with Diverse Competencies
- **Status**: Systematically integrated
- **Characteristics**:
  - Diverse teams required for all AI initiatives (policy requirement)

  - User experience expertise required in design
  - Participation documented in project records
  - Interdisciplinary collaboration prioritized and resourced
  - Team composition reviewed for adequacy

#### MAP 1.3: Organization's Mission and Goals for AI Technology
- **Status**: Clearly understood and systematically documented
- **Characteristics**:
  - AI goals explicitly aligned with organizational mission
  - Technology goals documented for all initiatives
  - Strategic alignment validated before project approval
  - Regular review of alignment throughout lifecycle

#### MAP 1.4: Business Value or Context of Business Use
- **Status**: Clearly defined and validated
- **Characteristics**:
  - Business cases required for all AI systems
  - Value quantified with specific metrics
  - Context evaluated regularly for all systems
  - ROI calculated and monitored
  - Value realization tracked post-deployment

#### MAP 1.5: Organizational Risk Tolerances
- **Status**: Formally determined and documented
- **Characteristics**:
  - Organizational risk tolerance formally defined
  - Risk appetite statements approved by governance
  - AI-specific risk tolerance levels established
  - Documented and communicated across organization
  - Risk tolerance informs all AI decisions

#### MAP 1.6: System Requirements and Socio-Technical Implications
- **Status**: Comprehensively elicited and understood
- **Characteristics**:
  - Requirements systematically elicited from all relevant AI actors
  - Comprehensive functional and non-functional requirements

  - Design decisions explicitly consider socio-technical implications
  - Requirements traceability maintained
