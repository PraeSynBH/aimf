## The single most important thing about this order

Executive Order 14179 was written to **remove** federal AI requirements, not to impose them. It revoked EO 14110 - the order that had directed safety testing and training-run reporting - and directed agencies to review and suspend, revise or rescind the actions taken under it. It creates no compliance duty of any kind for private-sector AI developers, deployers or vendors, and its general provisions state that it creates no right or benefit enforceable against the United States.

That makes the honest mapping posture unusual: the order itself is not a control set to conform to. What matters for an organisation is the **follow-on federal machinery** the order triggered and the obligations a supplier inherits through it - principally OMB M-25-21 and M-25-22 of 3 April 2025 and the AI Action Plan released on 23 July 2025. This document therefore maps provisions and follow-on actions to the AIMF controls that make an organisation ready, and labels the applicability of each to organisation type.

## Reading the order as an AI risk programme

Stripped of the revocation language, the order's operative content is a policy direction: sustain and enhance US leadership in AI, and remove what the administration regarded as obstacles to it. An organisation that wants to read its AIMF position against that direction should focus on three things the order changed:

### 1. Safety obligations moved from federal mandate to voluntary practice

EO 14110's testing and reporting duties no longer bind anyone. The controls that satisfied them (MEA-2.5 validity testing, MEA-2.6 safety assessment, MEA-2.11 fairness assessment, MAN-1.4 residual risk documentation) remain in the AIMF because they remain the substance of responsible AI practice, and they remain the evidence a regulated customer or a state regulator will ask for. The AIMF states this as a deliberate position rather than dropping the controls when the mandate went away.

### 2. Federal procurement became the transmission mechanism

Because the order removed government-wide AI requirements, the obligations that reach private-sector organisations now arrive through procurement: an agency buying AI must apply its own minimum practices, and it will push evidence expectations onto its suppliers. GOV-1.7 (third-party management), MAP-4.1 and MAP-4.2 (third-party risk assessment and controls) and MAN-3.1 (third-party monitoring) are the controls that put an organisation in a position to answer those questions.

### 3. The reporting obligation moved to the state and sector regulators

The federal reporting threshold no longer exists. State AI legislation and sector regulators (financial services, health, employment) are the more probable near-term source of AI obligations for a private-sector organisation. The AIMF's governance controls (GOV-1.1 legal and regulatory compliance, GOV-1.4 compliance verification) are deliberately written at the level of "applicable AI law and regulation" rather than a named instrument, so the framework does not need re-cutting every time the statutory landscape moves.

## Maturity progression against federal readiness

| AIMF level | Federal-supplier readiness posture |
|---|---|
| Level 1 - Initial | No awareness of AI-specific procurement expectations; no AI policy, no impact assessment, no AI inventory |
| Level 2 - Developing | Basic AI policy and use-case inventory exist; AI risk assessed for in-scope systems; named owner for AI |
| Level 3 - Defined | Impact assessment performed before high-impact deployment; pre-deployment testing documented; human oversight and operator training in place - the posture M-25-21 describes for agencies |
| Level 4 - Managed | Monitoring, metrics and periodic re-assessment running; evidence produced on demand for a procurement review |
| Level 5 - Optimizing | The organisation contributes to the standards and practice its regulators and customers will adopt |

## Maintenance

This document's tables are generated. The hand-authored analysis above is maintained in this file; the provision-to-control attribution and the applicability table live in `scripts/aimf_mappings.py`. To change the mapping, edit `EO14179_ACTIONS` or `EO14179_PRIVATE_SECTOR` in that script and re-run:

```
python3 scripts/aimf_mappings.py generate
python3 scripts/aimf_mappings.py check
```

`check` fails if an EO 14179 mapping cites an AIMF control id that does not exist in the catalog, and if the provision table falls below ten entries (the order plus its three follow-on actions).
