## Getting Started

### What is the AI Management Framework (AIMF)?

AIMF is a comprehensive, standards-based framework for assessing, developing, and improving organizational AI governance capabilities. It provides:
- 72 controls aligned with ISO 42001 and NIST AI RMF
- 5 maturity levels (Initial → Optimizing) across 4 capability dimensions
- 3 implementation tiers (B1, B2, B3) based on risk levels
- 6 framework profiles for different organizational contexts
- Complete standards integration (ISO 42001, NIST AI RMF, ISO 27001, Executive Orders)

**Key Differentiators**:
- **Practical implementation**: Tier-specific requirements with clear acceptance criteria
- **Profile-based approach**: Customizable starting points for different organizations and industries
- **Maturity progression**: Clear pathways from initial AI governance to optimized programs
- **Open framework**: Free to use under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 license

---

### How does AIMF relate to other frameworks?

AIMF is designed as a **practical implementation guide** for existing standards:

| Standard | Relationship |
|----------|--------------|
| **ISO 42001** | AIMF maps all 72 controls to ISO 42001 clauses; can be used for ISO 42001 certification preparation |
| **NIST AI RMF** | AIMF's 4 functions (GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE) align with NIST AI RMF; all 72 NIST subcategories mapped |
| **ISO 27001** | AIMF security controls map to ISO 27001 Annex A; complementary for AI + cybersecurity governance |
| **Executive Orders 14110/14179** | AIMF profiles address EO requirements for federal agencies and AI safety reporting |
| **EU AI Act** | High-Assurance profile supports high-risk AI requirements; compliance mappings in development |

**Think of it this way**: NIST AI RMF and ISO 42001 define "what" (requirements), AIMF defines "how" (implementation with tiers, profiles, and maturity progression).

## Framework Concepts

### What's the difference between maturity levels, tiers, and profiles?

These are three complementary dimensions of AIMF:

**Maturity Levels (1-5)**: **WHERE you are**
- Assess organizational capability: Initial → Developing → Defined → Managed → Optimizing
- Applied across 4 dimensions: Governance, Data, Technical, Culture
- **Use**: Self-assessment to understand current state and set improvement targets

**Risk Baselines (B1, B2, B3)**: **HOW rigorously you implement**
- B1 (Initial): Basic implementation for low-risk AI
- B2 (Mature): Systematic implementation for moderate-risk AI
- B3 (High-Risk): Maximum rigor for high-risk/critical AI
- **Use**: Select appropriate implementation depth based on AI system risk

**Profiles**: **WHICH controls you implement**
- Framework profiles: Pre-defined control selections (Foundational, Balanced, Healthcare, etc.)
- Implementation profiles: Your organization's customized control selection
- **Use**: Tailor AIMF to your industry, risk profile, and regulatory requirements

**Example**: A mid-market healthcare company might use the Healthcare Profile (which controls), implement at B2 tier (how rigorous), and currently be at Maturity Level 2 (where they are), targeting Level 3 (where they want to be).

This depends on your profile selection and risk assessment:

| Profile | Controls | Coverage | Typical Use Case |
|---------|----------|----------|------------------|
| **Foundational** | 35 | 48% | Minimum viable governance; low-risk AI |
| **Balanced** | 50 | 68% | Standard enterprise AI governance |
| **High-Assurance** | 72 | 100% | Maximum rigor for high-risk AI |
| **Financial Services** | 59 | 81% | Regulated financial institutions |
| **Healthcare** | 62 | 85% | Patient-facing and clinical AI |
| **Government** | 63 | 86% | Public sector with transparency requirements |

**You can customize**: Start with a framework profile, then add/remove controls based on your specific context. Document deviations with risk acceptance decisions.

**Recommendation**: Start with Foundational (establish basics), advance to Balanced (comprehensive program), then consider High-Assurance or industry profiles as needed.

### Can I implement AIMF incrementally?

Yes! AIMF is designed for phased implementation:

**Typical Phased Approach**:
1. **Phase 1 (Months 1-3)**: Governance Foundation
   - Establish AI governance committee
   - Document legal/regulatory requirements
   - Conduct initial AI system inventory and risk assessment
   - Implement essential GOVERN controls

2. **Phase 2 (Months 3-9)**: Comprehensive Assessment
   - Impact assessments for AI systems
   - Testing and validation procedures (MEASURE controls)
   - Stakeholder engagement program
   - Initial monitoring capabilities

3. **Phase 3 (Months 9-18)**: Operational Excellence
   - Continuous monitoring and improvement (MANAGE controls)
   - Incident response integration
   - Training and capability development
   - Regular audits and reviews

**Start small, prove value, expand**: Focus on high-priority controls or high-risk AI systems first, then expand coverage.

## Implementation Questions

### Do I need to hire a consultant to implement AIMF?

Not required, but may accelerate implementation:

**Self-Implementation** (Internal team only):
- **Advantages**: Lower cost, builds internal capability, full control
- **Best for**: Organizations with existing governance/compliance expertise, mature risk management programs
- **Timeline**: 9-18 months for Balanced Profile (longer for High-Assurance)
- **Resources**: Dedicated 2-4 FTE team (governance lead, compliance, technical)

**Consultant-Assisted**:
- **Advantages**: Faster implementation, expert guidance, external validation
- **Best for**: Organizations new to AI governance, complex regulatory requirements, limited internal expertise
- **Timeline**: 6-12 months with external support for Balanced Profile
- **Cost**: $50K-$300K depending on scope and consultant (ballpark estimates)

**Hybrid Approach** (Recommended):
- External consultant for Phase 1 (governance design, framework customization)
- Internal team for Phases 2-3 (implementation, ongoing management)
- External audit/validation at completion

---

### How long does AIMF implementation take?

Implementation timelines vary by profile, organization size, and existing maturity:

| Profile | Timeline | Assumptions |
|---------|----------|-------------|
| **Foundational** | 3-6 months | Small org, low-risk AI, dedicated 1-2 FTE |
| **Balanced** | 6-12 months | Mid-market to enterprise, moderate-risk, 2-4 FTE |
| **High-Assurance** | 12-24 months | High-risk AI, dedicated team, external experts |
| **Industry Profiles** | 9-18 months | Regulatory compliance driver, 3-5 FTE |

**Factors affecting timeline**:
- Existing maturity (higher starting maturity = faster)
- Organizational complexity (multiple business units, geographies)
- Number/complexity of AI systems
- Regulatory requirements (e.g., FDA approval for medical devices)
- Resource availability (dedicated vs part-time team)

**Accelerators**: Executive commitment, existing compliance programs (SOC 2, ISO 27001), mature MLOps practices, external consultants

**Common pitfall**: Underestimating stakeholder engagement and change management time

### What roles do I need for AIMF implementation?

Typical implementation team structure:

**Core Team** (Dedicated):
- **AI Governance Lead** (1 FTE): Overall program management, stakeholder coordination
- **Compliance/Legal** (0.5-1 FTE): Regulatory interpretation, policy development
- **Technical Lead** (1 FTE): MLOps integration, technical controls implementation
- **Quality/Risk** (0.5 FTE): Risk assessment, incident response, audits

**Extended Team** (Part-time/Advisory):
- **Executive Sponsor**: Budget approval, policy sign-off, organizational influence
- **Data Science/ML Engineers**: Model validation, fairness testing, technical input
- **IT/Security**: Infrastructure security, access controls, monitoring
- **Product Management**: Requirements, impact assessment, stakeholder feedback
- **Legal Counsel**: Contracts, liability, regulatory compliance
- **External Consultants** (optional): Framework design, industry expertise, validation

**Governance Committee** (Oversight):
- Cross-functional committee meeting monthly/quarterly
- Includes representatives from engineering, legal, product, security, risk
- Reviews AI governance decisions, risk acceptance, policy updates

## Technical Questions

### How does AIMF address generative AI (LLMs)?

Generative AI introduces unique risks addressed across AIMF controls:

**Key Control Areas**:
1. **Safety (MEA-2.6)**: Prompt injection, jailbreaks, harmful content generation
2. **Security (MEA-2.7)**: Model extraction, data poisoning, adversarial attacks
3. **Privacy (MEA-2.10)**: Training data memorization, personal information leakage
4. **Fairness (MEA-2.11)**: Bias amplification in generated content
5. **Transparency (MEA-2.8)**: Disclosure of AI-generated content
6. **Third-Party (MAP-4.1, MAN-3.1)**: Foundation model risks, API dependencies

**B3 Requirements for High-Risk Generative AI**:
- Red team security testing
- Adversarial robustness evaluation
- Content filtering and safety mechanisms
- User disclosures about AI generation
- Continuous monitoring for misuse patterns

**Future**: AIMF v1.1 will include a Generative AI use-case profile with expanded guidance.

---

### Does AIMF cover data privacy (GDPR, HIPAA)?

Yes, through MEA-2.10 (Privacy Evaluation) and related controls:

**Privacy Controls**:
- **MEA-2.10**: Privacy impact assessment, data minimization, consent management
- **MAP-1.3**: Data governance policies aligned with privacy regulations
- **MEA-2.7**: Security safeguards (encryption, access controls)
- **GOV-1.1**: Legal compliance including GDPR, HIPAA, CCPA
- **MAP-3.3**: Data retention and deletion procedures

**Healthcare Profile** (HIPAA):
- Enhanced MEA-2.10 requirements for PHI-processing AI
- HIPAA Privacy Rule and Security Rule compliance
- Business Associate Agreements for AI vendors
- Breach notification procedures

**Government Profile** (Privacy Act, FOIA):
- Transparency requirements for data use
- Public access to AI decision logic
- Civil rights protections

**Note**: AIMF provides AI-specific privacy controls; should complement existing privacy management programs, not replace them.

### How does AIMF handle third-party AI (vendors, open-source models)?

Third-party AI requires specialized controls:

**MAP Function** (Assessment):
- **MAP-4.1**: Third-Party AI Dependency Assessment
  - Evaluate vendor AI governance maturity
  - Assess model transparency and documentation
  - Identify lock-in risks and concentration
- **MAP-4.2**: FOSS AI Risk Management
  - Open-source model provenance and licensing
  - Community governance and support assessment

**MANAGE Function** (Ongoing Monitoring):
- **MAN-3.1**: Third-Party AI Monitoring
  - Continuous vendor performance tracking
  - Model update notifications and validation
  - Vendor audit rights and reviews
- **MAN-3.2**: Pre-trained Model Management
  - Foundation model lifecycle management
  - Fine-tuning governance
  - Drift detection for vendor models

**Contractual Safeguards**:
- AI-specific SLAs (performance, fairness, availability)
- Model documentation and explainability requirements
- Audit and validation rights
- Data usage and retention provisions
- Liability and indemnification for AI failures

**Risk Tiers**:
- B1: Basic vendor assessment, standard contracts
- B2: Documented vendor risk management, audit rights
- B3: Independent vendor validation, continuous monitoring, contractual audit enforcement

Yes, AIMF is explicitly designed to support ISO 42001 certification:

**Direct Alignment**:
- All 72 AIMF controls map to ISO 42001 clauses
- Control catalog includes ISO 42001 clause references
- AIMF Assessment Guide can serve as ISO 42001 internal audit procedure

**Using AIMF for ISO 42001 Certification**:
1. **Gap Analysis**: Use AIMF maturity assessment to identify ISO 42001 gaps
2. **Implementation**: Use AIMF controls and tier requirements as ISO 42001 implementation guide
3. **Documentation**: AIMF control evidence serves as ISO 42001 documentation
4. **Internal Audit**: AIMF assessment methodology for ISO 42001 compliance audits
5. **Certification Audit**: Present AIMF implementation profile as evidence of ISO 42001 compliance

**Balanced Profile = ISO 42001 Core Requirements**:
- Balanced Profile (50 controls at B2) covers essential ISO 42001 clauses
- High-Assurance Profile (72 controls at B3) addresses all optional/recommended ISO 42001 controls

**Certification Readiness**: Organizations implementing AIMF Balanced or High-Assurance profiles should be well-positioned for ISO 42001 certification audit.

### Does implementing AIMF guarantee regulatory compliance?

No, but it significantly supports compliance efforts:

**What AIMF Provides**:
- ✅ Comprehensive control framework aligned with regulatory expectations
- ✅ Standards integration (ISO 42001, NIST AI RMF, ISO 27001)
- ✅ Industry profiles addressing sector-specific regulations
- ✅ Documentation and evidence for compliance audits

**What AIMF Does Not Provide**:
- ❌ Legal advice or regulatory interpretation
- ❌ Guarantee of compliance with evolving regulations
- ❌ Replacement for legal counsel or compliance officers
- ❌ Automatic approval from regulators

**Recommended Approach**:
1. Use AIMF to establish AI governance structure
2. Engage legal counsel to interpret specific regulatory requirements
3. Map regulations to AIMF controls (many mappings provided)
4. Implement controls at appropriate tier based on regulatory risk
5. Document compliance evidence through AIMF implementation
6. Engage with regulators proactively to demonstrate governance maturity

**Regulatory Confidence**: Organizations implementing AIMF can demonstrate systematic AI risk management to regulators, auditors, and stakeholders.

## Troubleshooting

### Our organization is stuck at Maturity Level 2. How do we advance?

Common challenges advancing from Level 2 (Developing) to Level 3 (Defined):

**Typical Blockers**:
1. **Ad-hoc processes**: Governance exists but not formalized or consistently applied
   - **Solution**: Document processes, create templates, establish systematic procedures
2. **Limited resources**: Part-time governance team, competing priorities
   - **Solution**: Secure dedicated FTE, demonstrate value through quick wins, phase implementation
3. **Resistance to process**: Engineers view governance as bureaucracy
   - **Solution**: Automate controls, integrate with existing workflows, show efficiency gains
4. **Lack of executive support**: Governance seen as IT problem, not strategic priority
   - **Solution**: Frame AI risk in business terms, demonstrate regulatory/reputational risks, benchmark competitors

**Level 2 → Level 3 Transition Guide**: See `MATURITY-LEVEL-2-TO-3-GUIDE.md` for detailed roadmap

**Key Success Factors**:
- Executive sponsorship (not just budget, but active engagement)
- Integration with existing programs (risk management, compliance, quality)
- Metrics demonstrating governance value (incidents prevented, efficiency gains, compliance readiness)
- Cultural change management (not just policy, but behavior change)

### How do I handle conflicts between different fairness metrics?

Fairness metrics often conflict - optimizing for one can worsen another. AIMF addresses this through:

**MEA-2.11 (Fairness Assessment) Guidance**:
1. **Stakeholder Engagement (MAP-5.2)**: Identify which fairness criteria matter to affected communities
2. **Context-Specific Selection**: Choose fairness metrics appropriate to use case:
   - **Lending**: Equal opportunity (equal true positive rates across groups)
   - **Hiring**: Demographic parity (equal positive rates across groups)
   - **Healthcare**: Calibration (predicted risk = actual risk for all groups)
3. **Multi-Metric Evaluation**: Report multiple fairness metrics, acknowledge tradeoffs
4. **Explainability (MEA-2.9)**: Explain fairness choices to stakeholders
5. **Continuous Monitoring**: Track fairness metrics in production, reassess periodically

**B3 Requirement**: Independent fairness assessment with stakeholder validation of metric selection

**No Universal Answer**: Fairness is context-dependent and requires value judgments, not just technical optimization. AIMF ensures these decisions are made transparently with stakeholder input.

---

### Our vendor won't provide model documentation. What do we do?

Common challenge addressed by MAP-4.1 (Third-Party AI Assessment) and MAN-3.1 (Ongoing Monitoring):

**Options**:
1. **Contract Negotiation**: Include AI documentation requirements in contracts
   - Model cards, training data provenance, performance metrics
   - Audit rights for high-risk AI
   - Notification of model updates with validation data
2. **Risk Acceptance**: If vendor won't provide documentation, document residual risk
   - Require executive risk acceptance
   - Implement compensating controls (additional validation, human oversight)
   - Consider alternative vendors for future
3. **Black-Box Validation**: Test vendor AI as a black box
   - Input/output testing across diverse scenarios
   - Fairness testing with representative data
   - Performance monitoring in production
   - Behavioral testing for edge cases
4. **Regulatory Escalation**: For high-risk AI in regulated industries, consider:
   - Engaging regulator for guidance on undocumented vendor AI
   - Declining to use vendor AI if documentation insufficient for compliance
   - Seeking alternative vendors with better transparency

**B2/B3 Requirement**: Vendor AI used in moderate/high-risk applications must have sufficient documentation for validation. If vendor refuses, escalate or accept documented risk.

## Future of AIMF

### Will AIMF be updated? How often?

Yes, AIMF will evolve with AI technology and regulation:

**Version Strategy**:
- **v1.x (Minor Updates)**: Every 6-12 months
  - Add new framework profiles (e.g., Generative AI, Manufacturing, Energy)
  - Refine control guidance based on implementation feedback
  - Update regulatory mappings (EU AI Act, state laws)
- **v2.x (Major Updates)**: Every 2-3 years
  - Incorporate new standards (ISO revisions, NIST updates)
  - Restructure controls if needed based on industry evolution
  - Add new capability dimensions or maturity levels if warranted

**Compatibility**: Minor updates (v1.x) maintain backward compatibility. Organizations on v1.0 can upgrade to v1.1 without major rework.
